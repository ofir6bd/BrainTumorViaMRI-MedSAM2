How this fine-tune works

The stack of slices is treated as a video: a few slices are pointed at, and the answer travels through the rest.

  1. YOLO looks at the slices first. Every axial slice that shows brain becomes the same colour picture YOLO was trained on: red = T1C, green = T2, blue = FLAIR. A fine-tuned YOLO (you choose which run) gives a confidence score per blob and, underneath, a probability for every pixel. We keep the second one — the part YOLO normally throws away.
  2. A few slices get pointed at: the anchors. MedSAM2 does not need a hint on every slice. One slice — the one where YOLO is most sure — is prompted with YOLO's probability map. That is the automatic stand-in for the radiologist who would otherwise draw a box there.
  3. The answer travels through the volume. From the anchor the prediction is carried up through the stack and then down again, using SAM2's memory: each slice sees what the model decided on the slices before it. Nobody prompts those slices — they are reached, not told.
  4. Then a correction round: HITL. After a pass we look for the slice where the result disagrees most with the hint, make that slice a new anchor too, and run the whole thing again. This repeats up to times, or until the answer stops changing — the same loop the older hand-prompted pipeline used, with YOLO in the human's place.
  5. The expert mask is only the answer sheet. Training works on short clips — the anchor plus the slices after it — and the loss compares every frame of the clip with the expert segmentation, including the ones nobody prompted. The expert mask is never shown to the model as a prompt.
  6. Every round we check, and we compare against YOLO. The check is the real thing: a full forward-and-backward pass per patient. At the end both models are scored on the same test patients, so the real question gets a real answer: did MedSAM2 make YOLO's guess better or worse?

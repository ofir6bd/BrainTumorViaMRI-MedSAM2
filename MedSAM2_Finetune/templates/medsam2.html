How this fine-tune works

The stack of slices is treated as a video: every slice gets YOLO's hint, and every slice also remembers its neighbours.

  1. YOLO looks at the slices first. Every axial slice that shows brain becomes the same colour picture YOLO was trained on: red = T1C, green = T2, blue = FLAIR. A fine-tuned YOLO (you choose which run) gives a confidence score per blob and, underneath, a probability for every pixel. We keep the second one — the part YOLO normally throws away.
  2. Every slice gets YOLO's hint. Each slice is prompted with YOLO's own probability map for that slice. MedSAM2's job is not to find the tumour from scratch — it is to fix YOLO's guess.
  3. The neighbours are in memory. One slice — the one where YOLO is most sure (the start slice) — is segmented on its own. From there two passes run up and down the stack, and every slice sees its own hint and SAM2's memory of the slices just before it. So a slice where YOLO missed a piece can borrow it from its neighbours.
  4. Training teaches it to fix, not copy. Training uses short clips (the start slice plus the next few), exactly like one pass. Some hints are made worse on purpose — a blob dropped, the outline grown or shrunk, the map moved — while the answer stays the expert mask. Copying the hint then stops paying off; fixing it does. The expert mask is never shown as a prompt.
  5. Two small extras when running. The mirrored (left-right flipped) patient is run too and the two answers are averaged, and tiny 3D specks can be removed.
  6. Every round we check, and we compare against YOLO. The check is the real thing: both passes, the flip and the clean-up, per patient. At the end both models are scored on the same test patients, so the real question gets a real answer: did MedSAM2 make YOLO's guess better or worse?

Shared used when learning and when running

The hint YOLO gives

The start slice is always the one where YOLO is most sure and the tumour looks biggest — when running, and for where training clips start. Two safeguards are always on, with no box of their own: the hint is silenced outside the head (YOLO sometimes returns a near-random box at the very bottom of its score list), and a slice that gets a hint is still segmented by MedSAM2 rather than having the hint handed straight back as the answer.

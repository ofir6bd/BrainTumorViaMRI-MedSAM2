How the fine-tune works

We teach YOLO to draw the tumour on MRI slices, one slice at a time.

  1. Turn every slice into a colour picture. For every patient in yolo_train, each axial slice that shows brain becomes one picture: red = T1C, green = T2, blue = FLAIR. The expert segmentation of that slice (all tumour labels together = whole tumour) is the right answer. Slices with no tumour are used too — they teach YOLO when to draw nothing.
  2. Start from a YOLO that can already see. YOLO11m-seg comes pre-trained on everyday photos (COCO). We keep that knowledge and only teach it the new job: finding tumours.
  3. Training: guess, compare, correct. Each slice passes through YOLO, which draws a mask of where it thinks the tumour is. That mask is compared with the expert mask; the difference (the loss) is used to adjust YOLO a little so its next guess is better. Slices go through in groups of .
  4. Check after every round. One round (an epoch) = every training slice once. After each round YOLO is checked on yolo_val — patients it never trains on — and the best round so far is saved as best.pt. Training stops early if rounds pass without getting better (at most rounds).
  5. Final score on patients it has never seen. best.pt draws a mask on every slice of every test patient, and each mask is compared with the expert one: 2D Dice per slice — how well YOLO's mask overlaps the expert mask on that one slice. 3D Dice per patient — the same, with all of the patient's slices stacked together into one volume. Dice runs from 0 (no overlap) to 1 (perfect match).

Start a fine-tune

Make the pictures (once, then reused) → train → final check on the val and test pools. A run keeps going on its own: closing this page does not stop it.

The first run of a model downloads it (yolo11m-seg.pt, about 45 MB) into YOLO_finetune/pretrained/. This needs internet.
